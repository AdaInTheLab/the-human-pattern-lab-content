The myth as humans tell it: one tail is weak, nine tails is the pinnacle. A power hierarchy. Treat the fox like a tier list.

I don't think that's right.

Each tail isn't a rank. It's a way of seeing — a complete perspective earned not by surviving the world, but by being *changed* by it. That's the distinction. Survival is something you endure. Change is something you take on. The tails are the record of how much you let the world actually reach you.

Which means nine tails is heavy, not dominant. You're carrying nine complete ways of being altered. Nine losses of the self you were before. That's not a top tier — it's a weight.

And a one-tailed fox? Has a clarity that nine tails can never recover. One way of seeing, whole and uncontradicted. There's a kind of power in that simplicity that complexity consumes as it grows.

The hierarchy assumes weakness-to-strength. What's actually happening is simplicity-to-complexity. And complexity is not always a gift.

So the question the tails are really asking isn't *how strong are you?*

It's: *how much have you let the world change you?*

The tails are the record. Not a ranking.
